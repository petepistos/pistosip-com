The rules you answer to

One baseline, many frameworks.

The SMB Control Baseline is built on the CIS Controls, which line up with the frameworks your regulators, carriers and customers ask about. Do the work once and it counts toward each of them.

Frameworks that include:

  • NY DFS Cybersecurity Regulation (23 NYCRR 500)
  • HIPAA Security Rule
  • NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC 2.0
  • ISO/IEC 27001
  • SOC 2
  • PCI DSS
  • NACHA
  • Shared Assessments SIG
Risk assessment

One score, worked out from the evidence.

Every safeguard in the SMB Control Baseline, grouped by topic, each marked Met, Not met or No evidence.

  • Your score is the share of applicable safeguards you meet, shown against a target, with counts of met, not met and not applicable.
  • Each safeguard that falls short says why in one line: which scan failed, which form is unsigned, which task has not started.
  • Safeguards that do not fit your company are marked not applicable and left out of the score.
  • Filter by topic, status or kind of evidence to see just what you need.
Risk register

What you have not fixed, and what you have decided to live with.

Every safeguard you do not meet lands here automatically and stays until someone fixes it or writes down why the company accepts it.

  • The register updates itself from the risk assessment, so the two never disagree.
  • Each risk is rated for likelihood and impact on a 1 to 5 scale and sorted into High, Medium or Low.
  • The starting rating is only a starting point. Open a risk with Decide to rate it for your company, name an owner, and record the decision.
  • You can add risks of your own alongside the ones PCS finds.
Run a scan

The evidence comes from looking, not from asking.

Twice a year. Each scan feeds the safeguards it checks, and a result counts for 180 days before it reads as stale.

  • Microsoft 365: Pistos checks your tenant settings from our server.
  • External: a light outside check of your web address, including the settings that stop others sending email as you.
  • Windows computers: pushed to every laptop through Intune; PCS watches for each result.
  • Business applications: looks at each application’s sign-in page from outside, the way a browser does. No passwords, nothing submitted.
  • Host information: run by hand when a single computer needs a closer look.
  • Scans that do not apply to a cloud-only company, such as Active Directory and Linux, are switched off and say why.

From finding to fix, in one click.

Every scan writes a report in plain English. Each failed check says what was found, what it means for the business, and carries an Open the step link to the exact instructions that fix it. Fix it, re-scan, and the safeguard turns to Met on the risk assessment.

Training

Training people finish, and proof that they did.

Narrated, illustrated modules your people actually watch to the end, recorded against each person as evidence.

  • A short beginner set is required for everyone. Intermediate and advanced courses are there for those who want more.
  • “How it works” explainers and documentaries tell the story behind real attacks, such as the rise and fall of the Silk Road.
  • Quiz games that make the lessons stick.
  • Each completion is dated and filed against the training safeguard, so “are your people trained?” has an answer with names on it.
