Implementation Guide

Implementing Intune in a Microsoft 365 Office: MSP Edition

Technical runbook for the engineer doing the work. One directory, remote admin in hand, scripted where it should be, with the evidence an examiner will ask for.

Audience: MSP engineers and vCISOsEnvironment: Microsoft 365 Business PremiumDevices: Company-owned Windows, Entra joinedVersion: 1.0

The client runs Microsoft 365 Business Premium and you hold remote administrative access to their endpoints through your own tooling. Identity, mail and devices already share one directory, so this is the short version of the project: enroll, baseline, gate, deliver, prove. Every device in scope ends up Entra joined, not registered, and every change leaves a per-device record.

Why Intune

Every Microsoft 365 business plan includes a small device management feature called Basic Mobility and Security. It can wipe a lost phone and set a few rules on it, and that is roughly where it stops. Intune, which comes with Microsoft 365 Business Premium, is the full version. If the business is on Business Standard today, it has the first column. Moving to Business Premium gives it the second, together with Defender for Business and the sign-in controls that make the second column enforceable.

CapabilityMicrosoft 365 without IntuneWith Intune (Business Premium)
Device health rulesA short fixed list, mostly aimed at phones.Full compliance policies for Windows: encryption, antivirus, firewall, OS version, and anything else you need.
Keeping unhealthy machines outNot available for Windows computers.A Windows machine that fails its health check is blocked from email, files and Teams until it is fixed. Automatic, no human in the loop.
Security standardA handful of settings.Microsoft's security baseline, several hundred hardening settings kept current as Windows changes, applied in one step.
Installing and removing softwareNo.Full software deployment with install, uninstall, detection rules and per-machine reporting.

Antivirus managementWhatever is on the machine.Defender for Business, included in Business Premium, managed from the same console. Scans can be started remotely.

The license line

Intune is included in Microsoft 365 Business Premium. It is not in Business Basic or Business Standard, and buying Intune on its own costs more than the difference between the plans. If the business is on Standard, the first step is to move the licenses to Premium, which also brings Defender for Business and the Conditional Access feature this guide relies on.

Conventions in this guide

Commands are shown in lowercase. PowerShell does not care about case for cmdlets, parameters or paths. Where a value must keep its case, such as a permission scope name or an installer property, it does. Every script here is meant to be read before it is run, and run against one machine before it is run against a fleet. Replace example.com, group names and paths with your own.

Assumptions

  • Company-owned Windows 10/11 Pro or Enterprise. Windows Home is a documented exception, out of scope for join and for the management extension.
  • You hold Global Administrator in the client tenant under an individually named account with its own MFA, and your remote access tool (Splashtop or equivalent) is in the client's written access agreement with named technician accounts and session logging.
  • Business Premium per user. Intune Plan 1, Entra ID P1, Defender for Business. Remediations, Endpoint Privilege Management and Remote Help are not licensed and nothing here depends on them.
  • Users already sign into Windows with their work account, or will after the pilot. Where a machine is on a local or personal account, Step 4 covers the join.

The ten steps

1

Tenant access, licenses, and a Graph session you can trust

Why this matters

Everything after this is scriptable. Nothing is scriptable until you can authenticate as the right principal in the right tenant and see the license pool.

What you do
  • Sign in to intune.microsoft.com as your named GA. Confirm Tenant administration > Tenant status shows MDM authority = Microsoft Intune.
  • If the client is on Business Standard, the licenses move to Premium before anything else. Intune is absent from Standard, and Basic Mobility and Security will not do what this guide needs.
  • Connect Graph, verify the tenant, check the pool.
# one-time: install the graph module for the current user
install-module microsoft.graph -scope currentuser -force

# connect with the scopes this guide uses
connect-mggraph -scopes "User.ReadWrite.All","Group.ReadWrite.All","Directory.ReadWrite.All","DeviceManagementManagedDevices.ReadWrite.All","DeviceManagementConfiguration.ReadWrite.All","Organization.Read.All"

# confirm which tenant you are in before doing anything else
get-mgcontext | select-object tenantid, account, scopes
get-mgorganization | select-object displayname, verifieddomains
# find the business premium sku and how many seats are free
get-mgsubscribedsku | select-object skupartnumber, consumedunits, @{n="total";e={$_.prepaidunits.enabled}}

# business premium's part number is SPB. capture its id.
$sku = (get-mgsubscribedsku | where-object skupartnumber -eq "SPB").skuid

# assign to one user. usage location must be set first or the assignment fails.
update-mguser -userid "jane@example.com" -usagelocation "US"
set-mguserlicense -userid "jane@example.com" -addlicenses @{skuid = $sku} -removelicenses @()

# assign to everyone in a group instead (preferred: licenses follow membership)
# entra admin center > groups > the group > licenses > assignments. not scriptable through this cmdlet.

# check who in scope is still unlicensed
get-mguser -all -property displayname,userprincipalname,assignedlicenses | where-object { $_.assignedlicenses.count -eq 0 } | select-object displayname, userprincipalname
How you know it worked

get-mgorganization returns the client's tenant, SPB shows enough free seats, no user in scope is unlicensed.

What goes wrong

Running the rest of this against your own tenant because the Graph session was cached. Print the context first, every time.

2

Scope the fleet, build the groups, create the break-glass

Why this matters

Every assignment targets a group. Build them before any policy exists. Device groups for anything that configures the machine; user groups only for things that follow the person. The break-glass account is created now so it exists before any policy that could lock you out.

What you do

Inventory first. One row per machine: hostname, user, ownership, edition, bucket.

BucketWhat it coversWhat it gets
Fully managedCompany-owned Windows Pro or Enterprise computersJoined, enrolled, compliance rules, baseline, everything in this guide
Protected, not managedPersonal computers and phones used for work, including any Windows Home machineApp protection on company apps and multifactor sign-in. The device itself stays untouched
Left aloneA consultant's own laptop, or a machine running software that cannot tolerate changeNothing, by deliberate decision, recorded with the reason and who approved it
# static security groups, device-based
$managed = new-mggroup -displayname "intune - managed windows" -mailenabled:$false -mailnickname "intune-managed-windows" -securityenabled -description "company-owned windows machines under full management"
$exclude = new-mggroup -displayname "intune - excluded devices" -mailenabled:$false -mailnickname "intune-excluded-devices" -securityenabled -description "machines deliberately kept out of policy. one row per exception, with the reason, in the ticket."
$pilot   = new-mggroup -displayname "intune - pilot" -mailenabled:$false -mailnickname "intune-pilot" -securityenabled -description "first machines to receive any new policy"

# optional: a dynamic group that picks up every company-owned windows device automatically
$rule = '(device.deviceOSType -eq "Windows") and (device.deviceOwnership -eq "Company")'
new-mggroup -displayname "intune - all corporate windows (dynamic)" -mailenabled:$false -mailnickname "intune-all-corp-windows" -securityenabled -grouptypes "DynamicMembership" -membershiprule $rule -membershipruleprocessingstate "On"

$managed.id; $exclude.id; $pilot.id
# create the emergency account. store the password offline, in two places, before you leave this screen.
$pw = -join ((48..57 + 65..90 + 97..122 + 33,35,36,37,38,42,64) | get-random -count 32 | foreach-object {[char]$_})
$bg = new-mguser -displayname "break glass" -userprincipalname "breakglass@example.onmicrosoft.com" -mailnickname "breakglass" -accountenabled -passwordprofile @{password = $pw; forcechangepasswordnextsignin = $false}

# global administrator role, assigned directly
$role = get-mgdirectoryrole | where-object displayname -eq "Global Administrator"
new-mgdirectoryrolememberbyref -directoryroleid $role.id -bodyparameter @{"@odata.id" = "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/directoryObjects/$($bg.id)"}

# now, in the entra admin center, exclude this account from every conditional access policy you create.
# register a hardware security key as its second factor. do not leave it with no method.
write-output "store this now: $pw"

Exclusions are always a group, never an assignment filter.

How you know it worked

Managed, excluded and pilot groups exist, the dynamic group is defined, one break-glass GA exists with a hardware key and an offline password.

What goes wrong

Mixing user and device groups across include and exclude on the same assignment. Intune evaluates them differently and the result is not what you drew on the whiteboard.

3

Enrollment configuration, restrictions, and Autopilot

Why this matters

Automatic enrollment turns an Entra join into an Intune enrollment with no second step. Enrollment restrictions keep personal devices out. Autopilot means the next laptop the client buys arrives already yours.

What you do
  • Devices > Enrollment > Windows > Automatic Enrollment. MDM user scope = Some, pilot group. MAM user scope = None. URLs at defaults.
  • Enrollment restrictions: block personally owned Windows. Company-owned only.
  • Add the two CNAMEs at the registrar so enrollment resolves cleanly from any network.
  • Register existing hardware for Autopilot so a reset puts it back in a managed state, and set up a deployment profile for new purchases.
# cnames at the registrar
# enterpriseenrollment.example.com    cname   enterpriseenrollment.manage.microsoft.com
# enterpriseregistration.example.com  cname   enterpriseregistration.windows.net

# autopilot: capture and register hardware hashes. run on each machine through your remote tool, elevated.
install-script get-windowsautopilotinfo -force
get-windowsautopilotinfo -online -grouptag "corp-windows" -assign

# or collect hashes to csv from machines without graph access, then import in the console
get-windowsautopilotinfo -outputfile "c:\temp\$env:computername-hash.csv"

Then Devices > Enrollment > Windows > Deployment profiles: user-driven, Entra joined, skip privacy and EULA pages, standard user (not administrator), naming template corp-%SERIAL%. Assign to the dynamic Autopilot group that the group tag creates.

How you know it worked

A pilot user signing into a fresh machine produces an Entra joined, enrolled device within minutes, and the Autopilot device list shows your hashes with the group tag.

What goes wrong

MDM user scope set to All on day one. Every machine anyone adds a work account to starts enrolling, including the owner's home PC.

4

Pilot: prove the join state, then force the sync

Why this matters

A machine already signed in with the work account may have joined and enrolled the moment you changed Step 3. A machine on a local or personal account has not, and needs the join done properly. Either way, you confirm from the device, not from the console.

What you do
  • Through your remote tool, open an elevated prompt on the pilot machine and check the join state.
# run on the machine, in an elevated prompt. the lines that matter:
#   AzureAdJoined : YES     -> joined. good.
#   AzureAdJoined : NO  with WorkplaceJoined : YES  -> registered only. policies will not land.
#   TenantName / TenantId  -> confirm it is the right tenant, not a personal one
#   MdmUrl                 -> populated means intune enrollment completed
dsregcmd /status

# the same thing, filtered, for pasting into a ticket
dsregcmd /status | select-string "azureadjoined|workplacejoined|tenantname|tenantid|mdmurl|devicename"
  • If AzureAdJoined : NO: back up the profile, then Settings > Accounts > Access work or school > Connect > Join this device to Microsoft Entra ID. Restart, sign in as the work account, migrate the profile. For more than a handful of seats, a profile migration tool is worth the license.
  • If joined, force the sync and confirm the device appears, corporate, with a recent check-in.
# force a check-in from the machine itself (elevated)
get-scheduledtask | where-object taskpath -like "\microsoft\windows\enterprisemgmt\*" | start-scheduledtask

# or from the console, by device name
$d = get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -filter "devicename eq 'pc-reception-01'"
sync-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -manageddeviceid $d.id

# every enrolled windows device at once
get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -all -filter "operatingsystem eq 'Windows'" | foreach-object { sync-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -manageddeviceid $_.id }

Leave it alone for several days of real work before any policy touches it.

How you know it worked

AzureAdJoined : YES, MdmUrl populated, right tenant ID, device shows Entra joined and corporate in the console with a recent sync.

What goes wrong

WorkplaceJoined : YES with AzureAdJoined : NO. Registered, not joined. Everything downstream silently fails to apply.

5

Compliance policy, five rules, seven-day grace

Why this matters

Compliance is the signal Conditional Access consumes. In a Microsoft shop that signal gates email, files and Teams, so get it green on the pilot before it gates anything.

What you do
# a starter windows compliance policy through graph. five rules, seven-day grace period.
$body = @{
  "@odata.type"                               = "#microsoft.graph.windows10CompliancePolicy"
  displayName                                 = "windows - baseline compliance"
  description                                 = "encryption, av, firewall, min os, password. seven day grace."
  bitLockerEnabled                            = $true
  antivirusRequired                           = $true
  antiSpywareRequired                         = $true
  defenderEnabled                             = $true
  firewallRequired                            = $true
  osMinimumVersion                            = "10.0.19045"
  passwordRequired                            = $true
  passwordMinimumLength                       = 12
  passwordRequiredType                        = "alphanumeric"
  activeFirewallRequired                      = $true
  scheduledActionsForRule = @(@{
    ruleName = "PasswordRequired"
    scheduledActionConfigurations = @(@{ actionType = "block"; gracePeriodHours = 168; notificationTemplateId = "" })
  })
}
$policy = invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/beta/deviceManagement/deviceCompliancePolicies" -body ($body | convertto-json -depth 6) -contenttype "application/json"

# assign to the pilot group, exclude the exclusion group
$assign = @{ assignments = @(
  @{ target = @{ "@odata.type" = "#microsoft.graph.groupAssignmentTarget"; groupId = $pilot.id } },
  @{ target = @{ "@odata.type" = "#microsoft.graph.exclusionGroupAssignmentTarget"; groupId = $exclude.id } }
)}
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/beta/deviceManagement/deviceCompliancePolicies/$($policy.id)/assign" -body ($assign | convertto-json -depth 6) -contenttype "application/json"

Set Devices > Compliance > Compliance policy settings > Mark devices with no compliance policy assigned as to Not compliant. Otherwise an unassigned device is treated as healthy and sails past Conditional Access.

How you know it worked

Pilot device reports Compliant, or names the failing rule. Either is a pass at this stage.

What goes wrong

A dozen rules on day one and a week of amber warnings. Five first, tighten later.

6

Security baseline, Windows LAPS, and the second profile

Why this matters

The Microsoft baseline is a few hundred settings you did not author and do not maintain. LAPS ends the shared local admin password. The intrusive settings go in a second profile so they can be rolled back without losing the baseline.

What you do
  • Endpoint security > Security baselines > Security Baseline for Windows 10 and later > Create profile. Review every section. Typical adjustments: password expiry at the owner's request (history still enforced), legacy protocols the AMS or carrier portal still needs.
  • Assign to pilot, exclude the exclusion group. Several days.
  • Endpoint security > Account protection > Local admin password solution: backup directory = Entra, rotate every 30 days, 20+ character complexity, post-authentication reset. Assign to the managed group. This is safe to assign broadly on day one; it does not change user experience.
  • Second settings catalog profile for the tightening (removable storage, local admin removal, application control). Name it for what it is. Do not assign yet.
  • Defender for Business onboards automatically through Intune once the Defender connector is on: Endpoint security > Microsoft Defender for Endpoint, confirm the connection and allow Defender to enforce endpoint security configurations.
# retrieve a laps password through graph when you need the local admin on a machine
$dev = get-mgdevice -filter "displayname eq 'pc-reception-01'"
invoke-mggraphrequest -method get -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/directory/deviceLocalCredentials/$($dev.deviceid)?`$select=credentials" |
  select-object -expandproperty credentials | select-object accountname, @{n="password";e={[text.encoding]::utf8.getstring([convert]::frombase64string($_.passwordbase64))}}, backupdatetime
# every retrieval is audited. that is the point.

The baselines mirror CIS and NIST closely and are not certified against either. Starting point, not control mapping.

How you know it worked

Baseline succeeded on the pilot with no conflicts, LAPS has rotated and you can retrieve the password, Defender shows the device onboarded, and the user's work is unaffected after several days.

What goes wrong

Baseline assigned fleet-wide on creation day. Something breaks and you are bisecting three hundred settings in production.

7

Deliver scripts and software with evidence

Why this matters

PCS and the scanner scripts, your remote access agent, and whatever the office needs, delivered once per device, run as SYSTEM where required, with a per-device result you can show an examiner. The detection rule on each Win32 app is also your inventory of where that software exists.

What you do

Scripts. Platform scripts run once per device. For anything recurring, the script installs a scheduled task running as SYSTEM and exits. Remediations would do this natively and are not licensed on Business Premium.

# deploy this as an intune platform script (devices > scripts > add > windows 10 and later).
# run as: system. 64-bit: yes. signature check: yes once you have signed it.
# it drops the real worker script to disk and registers a daily task that runs it as system.
# intune runs this installer once per device. windows task scheduler does the recurring part.

$root = "c:\programdata\pistos\pcs"
new-item -path $root -itemtype directory -force | out-null

# lock the folder down: system and administrators only. standard users must not be able to edit what runs as system.
$acl = get-acl $root
$acl.setaccessruleprotection($true, $false)
$sys = new-object system.security.accesscontrol.filesystemaccessrule("nt authority\system","fullcontrol","containerinherit,objectinherit","none","allow")
$adm = new-object system.security.accesscontrol.filesystemaccessrule("builtin\administrators","fullcontrol","containerinherit,objectinherit","none","allow")
$acl.setaccessrule($sys); $acl.setaccessrule($adm)
set-acl -path $root -aclobject $acl

# write the worker. in production this is your signed scan script, pulled from your own distribution point.
@'
# pcs worker placeholder
$log = "c:\programdata\pistos\pcs\last-run.log"
"$(get-date -format o) ran as $env:username on $env:computername" | out-file $log -append
'@ | set-content -path "$root\scan.ps1" -encoding utf8

# register the task. system account, highest run level, daily at 02:00, run as soon as possible if missed.
$action    = new-scheduledtaskaction -execute "powershell.exe" -argument "-noprofile -executionpolicy bypass -windowstyle hidden -file `"$root\scan.ps1`""
$trigger   = new-scheduledtasktrigger -daily -at 2:00am
$principal = new-scheduledtaskprincipal -userid "nt authority\system" -logontype serviceaccount -runlevel highest
$settings  = new-scheduledtasksettingsset -startwhenavailable -executiontimelimit (new-timespan -hours 1) -multipleinstances ignorenew
register-scheduledtask -taskname "pistos pcs daily scan" -action $action -trigger $trigger -principal $principal -settings $settings -force | out-null

# tell intune it worked. non-zero exit shows as failed in the console.
if (get-scheduledtask -taskname "pistos pcs daily scan" -erroraction silentlycontinue) { exit 0 } else { exit 1 }

Software. Package as Win32 with a detection rule. Your remote access agent should be deployed this way too, so Intune reports where it is.

# package an installer for intune. one clean folder, the installer only, nothing else.
# the content prep tool is a free download from microsoft (intunewinapputil.exe).
.\intunewinapputil.exe -c .\source -s splashtop_streamer_deploy.msi -o .\output -q

# then in the console: apps > windows > add > windows app (win32), upload the .intunewin, and set:
#   install command:    msiexec /i "splashtop_streamer_deploy.msi" /qn /norestart USERINFO="hidewindow=1,confirm_d=0"
#   uninstall command:  msiexec /x {B7C5EA94-B96A-41F5-BE95-25D78B486678} /qn
#   install behavior:   system
#   detection rule:     registry, hklm\software\wow6432node\splashtop inc.\splashtop remote server, key exists
#   assignment:         required, to the device group. never to all devices.
# USERINFO stays uppercase: windows installer public properties are case-sensitive.

When something does not land.

# where the management extension writes its logs on the machine
get-childitem "c:\programdata\microsoft\intunemanagementextension\logs"

# the two that matter for scripts and win32 apps
get-content "c:\programdata\microsoft\intunemanagementextension\logs\intunemanagementextension.log" -tail 200
get-content "c:\programdata\microsoft\intunemanagementextension\logs\agentexecutor.log" -tail 200

# which scripts and apps the device thinks it has been given
get-childitem "hklm:\software\microsoft\intunemanagementextension\policies" -recurse | select-object pschildname
How you know it worked

Script shows Succeeded per device, the task exists and has run, the Win32 app shows Installed on the devices in the group and nowhere else.

What goes wrong

A scheduled task registered under a named local admin instead of SYSTEM. It stores the credential, breaks on LAPS rotation, and dies when the person leaves.

8

Conditional Access, report-only, with the break-glass excluded

Why this matters

In a Microsoft shop this gates everything: Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, and the admin portals you sign into. It is the control that makes the rest of the guide enforceable, and the one switch that can lock everyone out.

What you do
  • Confirm the break-glass from Step 2 has a hardware key registered and is excluded from every policy below.
  • If the tenant uses security defaults, you will replace them with explicit policies so the exclusion can exist. Do the policies in report-only first, then disable defaults, then switch the policies on.
  • Entra > Protection > Conditional Access. Policy 1: all users except break-glass, all cloud apps, require compliant device. Report-only.
  • Policy 2: all users except break-glass, all cloud apps, require MFA. Report-only.
  • Policy 3: all users except break-glass, block legacy authentication. Report-only.
  • Watch sign-in logs and the report-only results for a full week. Fix what would have been blocked.
  • Switch to On, one policy at a time, with the break-glass credentials open in a second browser.
# what would have been blocked in the last 7 days under report-only
$since = (get-date).adddays(-7).tostring("yyyy-MM-ddTHH:mm:ssZ")
get-mgauditlogsignin -filter "createdDateTime ge $since" -all |
  where-object { $_.conditionalaccessstatus -eq "reportOnlyFailure" } |
  select-object createddatetime, userprincipalname, appdisplayname, @{n="device";e={$_.devicedetail.displayname}}, @{n="compliant";e={$_.devicedetail.iscompliant}} |
  sort-object createddatetime -descending | format-table
How you know it worked

A compliant device signs in. A test device marked noncompliant is blocked with the expected message. Break-glass signs in from the blocked device. Legacy auth attempts fail.

What goes wrong

No exclusion, policy on, every admin locked out including the one that would undo it.

9

Roll out the fleet and use the remote actions you now have

Why this matters

With remote admin already in hand, the rollout is mostly a sequencing problem. Batch it, verify each machine from the device, and start using the remote actions that replace a site visit.

What you do
  • Widen MDM user scope from pilot to the managed group. Two or three seats a day. The Step 4 check on every one.
  • Remote actions from Graph, for the cases where the console is slower than a one-liner:
$d = get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -filter "devicename eq 'pc-reception-01'"
$base = "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/deviceManagement/managedDevices/$($d.id)"

invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/syncDevice"                 # check in now
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/rebootNow"                  # restart
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/remoteLock"                 # lock the screen
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/rotateBitLockerKeys"        # new recovery key
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/windowsDefenderScan" -body '{"quickScan": true}' -contenttype "application/json"
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/retire"                     # company data off, personal data stays
invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "$base/wipe" -body '{"keepEnrollmentData": false, "keepUserData": false}' -contenttype "application/json"   # lost or stolen only

# bitlocker recovery key for a device, when a user is locked out at the blue screen
$dev = get-mgdevice -filter "displayname eq 'pc-reception-01'"
invoke-mggraphrequest -method get -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/informationProtection/bitlocker/recoveryKeys?`$filter=deviceId eq '$($dev.deviceid)'" |
  select-object -expandproperty value | foreach-object {
    invoke-mggraphrequest -method get -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/informationProtection/bitlocker/recoveryKeys/$($_.id)?`$select=key"
  }
How you know it worked

Device count equals inventory, every row joinType = azureADJoined, compliance at or near 100 percent, and you have restarted at least one machine from Graph instead of driving there.

What goes wrong

Running wipe when you meant retire. Wipe is a factory reset. Retire removes company data and management. Read the line before you press enter.

10

Operate: reporting, leavers, and drift

Why this matters

Go-live is the start of the standing job. The fleet report is the artifact, the leaver procedure is the control, and the monthly review is what keeps the compliance number honest.

What you do

Monthly fleet report. Dated CSV into the client's evidence folder.

# fleet snapshot to csv. this is the artifact you hand to a carrier or examiner.
get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -all |
  select-object devicename, userprincipalname, operatingsystem, osversion, compliancestate,
                isencrypted, enrolleddatetime, lastsyncdatetime, managedDeviceOwnerType, joinType |
  sort-object compliancestate, devicename |
  export-csv -path ".\intune-fleet-$(get-date -format yyyy-MM-dd).csv" -notypeinformation

# just the problems
get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -all | where-object compliancestate -ne "compliant" |
  select-object devicename, userprincipalname, compliancestate, lastsyncdatetime

# machines that have not checked in for more than 14 days
get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -all | where-object { $_.lastsyncdatetime -lt (get-date).adddays(-14) } |
  select-object devicename, userprincipalname, lastsyncdatetime

Leavers. One function, run from a ticket, logged.

# leaver: one function, run from a ticket, logged.
function disable-leaver {
  param([parameter(mandatory)][string]$upn)
  $u = get-mguser -userid $upn -property id,displayname,userprincipalname
  update-mguser -userid $u.id -accountenabled:$false
  invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/users/$($u.id)/revokeSignInSessions"
  # retire their devices: company data off, personal data left alone. use wipe for company-owned hardware being recovered.
  get-mgdevicemanagementmanageddevice -filter "userprincipalname eq '$upn'" | foreach-object {
    invoke-mggraphrequest -method post -uri "https://graph.microsoft.com/v1.0/deviceManagement/managedDevices/$($_.id)/retire"
  }
  "$(get-date -format o) disabled $upn, sessions revoked, devices retired" | out-file ".\leavers.log" -append
}
disable-leaver -upn "leaver@example.com"

Monthly checks. Noncompliant and stale devices chased. Baseline change notes read. LAPS still rotating. Conditional Access report-only tab empty of surprises. Autopilot hashes present for every corporate machine so a reset returns it to management.

How you know it worked

Six months on: fleet count matches inventory, compliance green, every leaver disabled with a timestamp in the log, and the monthly CSVs line up in the evidence folder.

What goes wrong

Treating go-live as the finish line. Six months later half the fleet is noncompliant and nobody noticed, because after go-live nobody was looking.

The six mistakes that cause the most damage

  1. Assigning a policy to All devices. It is the default-looking option and it reaches machines you never intended, including the one you are working from.
  2. Skipping the pilot. Every hour saved here costs a day later, and it costs it in front of the whole office.
  3. No break-glass account excluded from Conditional Access. The one mistake that can lock you out of fixing the other five.
  4. Registering machines instead of joining them. They appear in the console and look managed, and nothing you deploy ever lands.
  5. One giant policy profile. It cannot be partly undone, so the first real problem turns into switching everything off.
  6. Using assignment filters for one-off exceptions. They work, they are invisible on the assignment page, and nobody remembers them six months on. Use an exclusion group.

Engagement checklist

  • Named GA in the client tenant with own MFA; remote access tool in the written access agreement with named technician accounts
  • Graph context verified against the right tenant; SPB seats sufficient; every user in scope licensed
  • Inventory with buckets signed off; managed, excluded, pilot and dynamic groups created
  • Break-glass GA created, hardware key registered, password offline in two places
  • MDM user scope = Some (pilot); personal Windows enrollment blocked; CNAMEs added; Autopilot hashes registered with a deployment profile
  • Pilot verified from the device: AzureAdJoined YES, MdmUrl populated; several days of normal use
  • Compliance policy on pilot; unassigned devices marked noncompliant
  • Baseline on pilot; LAPS on the managed group; intrusive settings in a separate unassigned profile; Defender connector on
  • PCS scheduled task script deployed as SYSTEM; remote agent and other apps as Win32 with detection rules
  • Conditional Access (compliant device, MFA, block legacy) report-only one week, then on; security defaults replaced; break-glass tested
  • Fleet joined in batches; dated fleet CSV in the evidence folder
  • Leaver function in use; monthly review scheduled with a named owner
